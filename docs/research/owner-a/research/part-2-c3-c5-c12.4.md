## Deviations from taxonomy

1. Citation title mismatch: the repo and SPEC.md files cite SRC-01 as "Watts This Smell: An Empirical Study on Energy Smells in Python Software". The arXiv abs page says "Watts This Smell: A Comprehensive Taxonomy of Software Energy Smells" (authors Mehditabar, Rajput, Sharma). The taxonomy is described as language-agnostic; only the validation data is Python/CPython. Fix the citation string.
2. How SRC-01 detected smells: not by any static tool. DeepSeek-V3.2 labelled 3,000 Pie-Perf pairs (top by energy difference) via an LLM pipeline (94% exact-match agreement on 100 samples). So SRC-01 gives no precision/recall for static detectors; it cannot support "our static rule reproduces the paper's finding".
3. C3 and C12 have no category-level savings numbers in the part of the paper read; the only quantified C3 item is C3.S7 (N=11, mean 9,000 J, one sieve example 23,446 J, 67x). The taxonomy row's "highest mean savings per instance in S01" is from that tiny N, and the paper itself attributes the sieve's root cause to algorithm choice (C7.S1) with the pop() as the quadratic trigger. The row's meaning ("modifying collections during iteration") does not describe the paper's example, which is `pop()` inside a `while`.
4. C5: the paper reports C5 median saving 3,989 J and 29.2% share of the top tier (chi-square p<0.001). It is category-level and LLM-labelled; no sub-root-cause stats (C5.S1-S4) were read. Do not attribute these numbers to specific root causes.
5. C12.4 "Native only": the paper excerpt reads C12.S4 absent from the data; I did not see the words "native only" in what I read (the claim may be in the unread final 11k characters; UNVERIFIED). Detection method "static stride analysis" is not applicable to Python.
6. Taxonomy detection signals "Profiler + static" for C3.1/C3.4/C5.1: practical existing tools are all static-only and style/micro-optimization oriented; Ruff states the PERF family "will have a negligible impact on performance in most cases", which is a caveat to weigh against calling these energy findings.
7. C3.4 (nested iteration) and C5.1 general list-membership-in-loop have no prior-art rule; "complexity analysis" is not practical beyond heuristics.
8. C5.2 prior-art rules (B019, W1518) point the other direction (caches can leak memory), so "add a cache" advice needs a guard.
9. Several PERF/FURB/B rules are preview in Ruff (B909, FURB148, FURB142, FURB113, PLR6201): they are flagged by Ruff as unstable, so relying on them as ground truth is risky.
