## 0. What SRC-01 actually says (arXiv 2604.04809, fetched abs + HTML)

- Abstract: 60 papers -> 320 patterns -> 12 smells, 65 root causes; validated by profiling >21,000 Python code pairs; 55 of 65 root causes mapped to code by an LLM pipeline. No mention of concurrency/hardware in the abstract.
- Table I definitions (HTML) match the taxonomy row "meaning" text exactly for C11.1-C11.5 and C12.1-C12.4 (e.g. "Overly broad synchronization serializing parallelizable work", "Leaked threads/tasks without clean termination", "Data-dependent branching in tight loops, defeating branch prediction").
- Sec. III-A says concurrency is essentially NOT validated: absent subcategories include "concurrency patterns (44 out of 55 C11 subcategories), requiring multi-threaded execution absent from single-threaded competitive code" (the "44 of 55" is internally inconsistent: Table I lists 5 C11 root causes; read as paper sloppiness, not as data). Sec. VI: dataset "focuses on single-threaded, algorithmic competitive programming", "explaining the low prevalence of C9 and C11". C12.S4 is absent ("C-extension specificity"). Whether C12.S1-S3 were observed is not stated per subcategory.
- SRC-01 gives NO detection method (no profiler, no hardware counter, no lint rule) for any C11/C12 item; "perf" is used only to measure the dataset's energy. The taxonomy "detection signal" and "AWS mapping" columns are therefore workbook-author additions, not SRC-01 claims.
- Magnitudes quoted by the paper: architecture-level (C9+C11, N=28) median 415 J; hardware-level (C12, N=61) median 561 J, both with the caveat that competitive-programming code under-represents them. Not usable as evidence that a given finding in a client repo saves energy.

## 3. Deviations from taxonomy / SRC-01

1. C11.x and C12.x "AWS mapping" assume "profile-artifact parser" can detect lock contention, serial bottlenecks, leaked threads, missed parallelism. py-spy and Scalene READMEs, as fetched, document neither lock-contention nor hardware-counter output. Mapping is not supported.
2. SRC-01 does not provide any detection signal; "Thread/async profiler", "Hardware counters" are workbook additions. SRC-01 itself says C11 is almost absent from its dataset (single-threaded competitive code) and gives no per-subcategory evidence for C12.S1-S3; C12.S4 is explicitly absent.
3. C11.4 row says "hard statically"; Ruff ASYNC210/220/230/250/251 show the `async def` subset is statically detectable. The row's "Python main thread" wording is also looser than "event-loop thread".
4. C11.3 "Leaked background threads": the nearest real rule (RUF006) is about tasks being garbage collected, not threads running forever; framing as energy waste is unsupported.
5. C11.5 and C11.1 rows carry the taxonomy's own warning "energy may not drop": do not report energy savings.
6. SRC-01 sentence "44 out of 55 C11 subcategories" is internally inconsistent with Table I (5 C11 root causes).
7. Workbook row C12.4 "Static stride analysis" is outside my assignment; not researched.
